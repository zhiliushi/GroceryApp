## 1. Trademark — full registration matrix

### 1.1 Why trademark is the highest-ROI IP for this launch
Software is auto-protected by copyright (§2). Patents are unrealistic for a pantry tracker (§4). The brand name + logo on the App Store / Play Store is what users recognise, what competitors can copy, and what the platforms enforce on behalf of registered owners (§8). Trademark is the only IP asset where pre-emptive registration buys real, enforceable, platform-recognised protection — and where the sole proprietor's first ringgits should go.

### 1.2 Nice classes for a SaaS / mobile app
For a downloadable+web pantry tracker, the canonical classes are ([USPTO Nice general remarks](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/trademark-updates-and-announcements/nice-agreement-current-edition-version-general-remarks); [TramaTM Class 9 guide](https://www.tramatm.com/class-assist/trademark-class/9); [JPG Legal software trademark guide](https://jpglegal.com/software-trademark-guide-classes-and-specimens/)):

| Class | Coverage | Needed for GroceryApp? |
|---|---|---|
| **9** | Downloadable mobile applications, downloadable software | **Yes** — covers the iOS / Android binary |
| **42** | SaaS / non-downloadable software, software design & development, cloud hosting | **Yes** — covers the FastAPI/Firestore backend exposed to users via app or web admin |
| **35** | Retail/business services, online ordering, marketplace, advertising | **Optional** — only if monetisation includes affiliate grocery purchase, marketplace, or paid promotions. Skip for a pure pantry tracker. |

**Recommendation:** File **Class 9 + Class 42** for every jurisdiction. Add Class 35 only if the freemium roadmap includes a marketplace or affiliate purchase flow within 12 months of filing (you can't add classes to an existing application — must file a new one).

### 1.3 Madrid Protocol routing — key correction to user assumption

**Indonesia IS a Madrid Protocol member** (effective **2 January 2018**, 100th member — [WIPO news](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/madrid-system/w/news/2017/news_0020); [Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/indonesia-joins-madrid-protocol/)). All in-scope jurisdictions (MY, SG, ID, TH, EU, US) are Madrid members, so a single MY-originated international application can designate all of them. As of March 2026 the Madrid Union has 116 members covering 132 countries ([WIPO Madrid System homepage](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/madrid-system)).

The catch: a Madrid international application **must be based on a "basic mark"** — a pending application or registration in the originating office (MyIPO). So Madrid is filed *after* (or simultaneously with) the MY filing, not instead of it.

Madrid is cost-effective when designating ≥3 jurisdictions; for 1–2 jurisdictions the Madrid basic fee + handling fee usually outweighs direct national filings.

### 1.5 What's protected: word vs logo vs combined
- **Word mark** (text only, no stylisation) — broadest protection across fonts, sizes, colours. **Best first filing** for an indie launch.
- **Figurative / device mark** (logo only) — protects the visual mark; doesn't protect the name.
- **Combined mark** (text + logo) — narrower because protection is tied to the specific composition.

**Recommendation:** File the **word mark** first (assuming the brand passes distinctiveness — see §9). Only file figurative versions later if the logo becomes a recognisable icon (e.g., the Spotify green-circle wave).

### 1.6 Strategic recommendation for indie launch budget

**Tier 1 — file before launch (mandatory):**
1. **Malaysia (Class 9 + 42)** — ~RM 1,900. Establishes the basic mark for Madrid downstream. Home jurisdiction; the cheapest enforceable footprint.

**Tier 2 — file before/during App Store submission:**
2. **EU (Class 9 + 42)** — €900 (~RM 4,500). Single filing covers 27 countries. The cheapest per-capita TM in scope. App Stores enforce EUIPO-registered marks across all EU storefronts.

**Tier 3 — file when traction in those markets is real:**
3. **Singapore (Class 9 + 42)** — S$560 (~RM 1,950). Cheap, fast, English-language, regional credibility for ASEAN expansion / investor due diligence.
4. **US (Class 9 + 42)** — ~RM 7,000 with attorney. **Defer until** US users >10% of MAU OR a US App Store competitor copies the brand. The §1(b) use requirement makes premature US filings risky.
5. **Indonesia / Thailand** — only if launching marketing campaigns there. Madrid via WIPO becomes attractive once 3+ designations are needed.

**Defer indefinitely:** WIPO Madrid until you actually need 3+ jurisdictions in one shot. Filing Madrid for just MY+EU costs more than two direct applications.

**ROI ranking for the cheapest first move:** MY → EU → SG. Total: **~RM 8,400 government fees** for trademark coverage of MY + 27 EU countries + Singapore = ~580 million people in trademark-policed markets.

## 2. Copyright

### 2.1 Auto-vesting under Berne Convention
Code, UI design, written content, marketing copy, screenshots, icons all get **automatic copyright protection on creation** in every Berne Convention country (which includes all in-scope jurisdictions). No registration required to *own* the copyright. ([Berne Convention overview at WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/treaties/textdetails/12214))

### 2.2 Per-jurisdiction registration

#### Malaysia
- **No mandatory registration system.** Auto-vests under Copyright Act 1987.
- **Voluntary Notification (Form CR-1)** under Copyright (Voluntary Notification) Regulations 2012 provides *prima facie* evidence of authorship — useful in litigation. Standard fee is RM 155 per work (literary/musical/film/sound recording/derivative). The fee was halved temporarily 24 Apr–24 Oct 2025 for the first 200 notifications under the Copyright (Reduction of Fee) Regulations 2025 ([MyIPO Notifying Copyright](https://www.myipo.gov.my/notifying-copyright/); [MyIPO 2025 fee reduction notice](https://www.myipo.gov.my/copyright-act-1987-notification-under-copyright-reduction-of-fee-regulations-2025/); [WIPO Lex on the regulations](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/15386)).
- **Recommendation:** **Skip** for source code (impractical to register per release; auto-vesting suffices in MY courts). **Do** notify the **logo + brand assets** post-launch — once the visual identity is finalised, one CR-1 filing of the logo set provides cheap evidentiary backing.

#### Singapore, EU, Indonesia, Thailand
- **No registration system.** Auto-vests. Keep dated source-control history (git commits, signed releases) as authorship evidence. ([IPOS copyright basics](https://www.ipos.gov.sg/understanding-innovation-ip/copyright))

#### United States — the only jurisdiction where registration buys real teeth
- Auto-vests, **but registration with the US Copyright Office is required to file an infringement suit** for US-origin works AND is the gateway to **statutory damages** ($750 to $30,000 per work, up to $150,000 for willful infringement) and attorney's fees. Without registration, you are limited to actual damages (often de minimis for an indie app).
- **Fee (eCO online)** — $45 single application (single author, same claimant, one work, not for hire); $65 standard application; $85 group registration of unpublished works ([US Copyright Office fees](https://www.copyright.gov/about/fees.html)).
- Timeline: 2–9 months to certificate; effective date is the date of submission.
- **For a MY developer, when does US registration pay back?**
  - If a US-based competitor clones the app → registered work qualifies for statutory damages + fees, making a takedown lawyer affordable contingency.
  - If the app is registered **before** infringement begins (or within 3 months of publication), statutory damages are available. Registration **after** infringement only allows actual damages.
  - **Threshold rule of thumb:** if US revenue or US-user share crosses 10% of total, register the **app code** and the **brand-asset bundle** (logo, marketing screenshots) as a $65 standard application each. Two filings, ~$130 total, buys statutory-damage standing.
- **What to register, not what to skip:** Register the **finalised release build** (deposit material can be the first 25 + last 25 pages of source code, with trade secrets redacted — [US Copyright Office Circular 61 on computer programs](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ61.pdf)). Re-register annually for major versions if litigation risk warrants.

### 2.3 Open-source contributions by the developer
If the sole proprietor (or any contractor) contributes to OSS projects under their own GitHub account, the contribution is governed by the OSS project's CLA / DCO. Personal contributions don't normally affect the company's IP — **except** when contributions touch code that overlaps with the proprietary product. In that case, code copied into the OSS contribution *and* the proprietary product creates a copyleft contamination risk.

**Hygiene rule:** keep a clear mental wall between OSS contributions and proprietary code. Don't copy/paste either direction. If hiring contractors later, every contract must include an explicit copyright assignment clause (§7).

### 2.4 Code license recommendation for own code
- **Keep proprietary.** No public licence. The repository is private; binary distribution via App Store / Play Store carries the standard end-user-licence terms set by Apple/Google for sub-licensing.
- **Copyright notice format** to include in every source file header (or at least in `LICENSE.txt`):
  ```
  Copyright (c) 2026 [Owner Name / Brand]. All rights reserved.
  Unauthorized copying, modification, distribution, or use is prohibited.
  ```
- This is belt-and-braces: copyright vests automatically, but the explicit notice removes any "innocent infringer" defence.

## 3. Open-source license compliance

This is the **highest probability of accidental legal trouble** for an indie launch — the team will pull dozens of npm + pip dependencies and may not realise some come with viral copyleft.

### 3.1 Common license families (npm + pip)

| License | Type | Distribution requirements (when shipping a binary or SaaS) |
|---|---|---|
| **MIT** | Permissive | Include the licence text and copyright notice in distribution. **No source-code disclosure.** |
| **Apache 2.0** | Permissive | Include licence + NOTICE file (if present); preserve attribution; declare significant changes. **No source-code disclosure.** Includes patent grant. |
| **BSD-2-Clause / BSD-3-Clause / ISC** | Permissive | Like MIT — include text + copyright. BSD-3 also restricts using the project name to endorse derivatives. |
| **MPL-2.0** | Weak copyleft (file-level) | Modifications to MPL-licensed files must be released under MPL. **Combining with proprietary code is OK** — the copyleft applies file-by-file, not to the whole work. |
| **LGPL-2.1 / LGPL-3** | Weak copyleft (library boundary) | OK to dynamically link from proprietary code. If you statically link, must allow user to relink the library. |
| **GPL-2 / GPL-3** | Strong copyleft | **Distribution of a combined work requires releasing the entire combined work under GPL.** Lethal for proprietary apps. |
| **AGPL-3** | Network copyleft | Same as GPL **plus**: providing access over a network counts as distribution. SaaS/back-end use of an AGPL component obligates you to publish your entire backend source under AGPL ([FOSSA AGPL guide](https://fossa.com/blog/open-source-software-licenses-101-agpl-license/); [GNU AGPL 3.0](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.en.html); [Mend SaaS loophole](https://www.mend.io/blog/the-saas-loophole-in-gpl-open-source-licenses/)). |
| **Unlicense / CC0 / WTFPL** | Public-domain dedication | Effectively no obligations. |

### 3.2 Practical impact on this stack

**FastAPI:** MIT — fine. **React Native:** MIT — fine. **Firestore SDK:** Apache 2.0 — fine. **Most of the JS ecosystem:** MIT/Apache/BSD — fine.

**Risks to actively scan for:**
- **GPL/AGPL** in any backend dependency (rare in npm/pip mainstream, but possible — e.g. some CLI tools, some database drivers, ghostscript-style binaries).
- **Linked-binary distribution issues** — bundling a GPL CLI tool inside the app via shell-out can create GPL contamination.
- **Reactive framework forks** licensed under SSPL or BSL (e.g., MongoDB SSPL — not OSI-approved; Elastic License v2 — restricts SaaS resale). Treat these as "do not use in our backend without legal review."

### 3.3 SBOM requirements

#### EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA)
- **Entered into force 10 December 2024.** ([European Commission CRA page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cyber-resilience-act))
- **Reporting obligations** (vulnerability + active-incident reporting) apply from **11 September 2026**.
- **Full obligations** including SBOM apply from **11 December 2027** ([HeroDevs CRA September 2026 timeline](https://www.herodevs.com/blog-posts/cra-reporting-obligations-start-september-2026-what-eol-dependencies-mean-for-your-compliance); [OpenSSF "Does CRA affect my business?"](https://openssf.org/blog/2025/02/20/does-the-eu-cra-affect-my-business/)).
- **Scope:** any "product with digital elements" placed on the EU market, including mobile apps. **A MY-developed app sold via App Store / Play Store with EU users is in scope.**
- **What's required:** machine-readable SBOM covering at least top-level dependencies, kept up to date, supplied to EU market-surveillance authorities on request (not necessarily public). Vulnerability handling, secure-by-design defaults, 24-hour notification of actively-exploited vulnerabilities.
- **Open-source carve-out:** non-monetised free open-source software is exempt; "open-source stewards" have lighter obligations. **Commercial mobile app does NOT qualify for the carve-out.**
- **Microenterprise consideration:** Article 26(1) requires the Commission to publish guidance "with particular focus on facilitating compliance by microenterprises and SMEs." Reduced documentation burden expected, but **not exemption**.
- **Penalties:** up to €15M or 2.5% of global turnover (so for an indie sole proprietor, the practical risk is being banned from EU storefronts, not catastrophic fines).

#### US Executive Order 14028
- Mandates SBOMs for **federal-government software procurement**. Does **not** apply to a consumer app on App Store / Play Store unless selling to a US government buyer.

#### Practical 2026–2027 plan for GroceryApp
- **Now (2026):** generate a CycloneDX SBOM as part of CI build. Costs nothing using open tooling; positions you for CRA reporting (Sep 2026) and full compliance (Dec 2027) without rework.
- **By Sep 2026:** establish a vulnerability-disclosure process (security.txt / a security@ email). CRA reporting clock starts.
- **By Dec 2027:** SBOM in technical documentation, secure-by-design defaults, conformity self-assessment.

### 3.4 Recommended tooling (free / open source)
- **`license-checker`** (npm) — `npx license-checker --production --summary` lists every transitive dep with its licence ([license-checker](https://github.com/davglass/license-checker)).
- **`pip-licenses`** (Python) — `pip-licenses --format=markdown --output-file=licenses.md`.
- **`@cyclonedx/cyclonedx-npm`** + `cyclonedx-py` — generate CycloneDX SBOM for npm and pip projects ([CycloneDX node-npm tool](https://github.com/CycloneDX/cyclonedx-node-npm); [CycloneDX Tool Center](https://cyclonedx.org/tool-center/)).
- **`ScanCode Toolkit`** — exhaustive licence + copyright scanner for any source tree ([ScanCode](https://scancode-toolkit.readthedocs.io/)).
- **`REUSE`** (FSF tool) — check that every source file has SPDX licence + copyright headers.

**FOSSA, Snyk, Sonatype** offer enterprise SaaS license-scan services; not needed for indie launch — open-source tools are sufficient.

### 3.5 In-app attribution screen — Apple/Google submission gate?
- **Apple:** App Store guidelines do not have a documented hard gate requiring an open-source notices screen ([Apple Developer Forum thread on OSS attribution](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/110635)). However, individual licence terms (MIT, Apache, BSD) do require attribution wherever the binary is distributed. Standard practice is a "Licenses" or "Acknowledgements" screen in Settings.
- **Google Play:** No documented hard gate for OSS attribution screens. Same per-licence obligations apply.
- **Best practice:** generate `LICENSES.md` / `Acknowledgements.txt` from `license-checker --json` output and ship it as an in-app screen accessible from Settings → About. Also include in the app's web admin footer.

### 3.6 License compatibility — combinations to avoid

| Combine | Outcome |
|---|---|
| MIT + Apache 2.0 | Fine. Distribute combined under more restrictive (Apache). |
| MIT + GPL | **Combined work effectively GPL.** Whole proprietary app must release source if any GPL code is statically linked. |
| MIT + AGPL | **Combined work effectively AGPL.** Backend source must be published if AGPL component is invoked over the network. |
| Proprietary + AGPL backend dep | **Trap.** The whole API must expose source. |
| Proprietary + LGPL dynamically linked | OK if you can satisfy the relinking requirement. |
| Proprietary app + MIT/Apache/BSD/ISC dep | OK with attribution. |

**Rule:** treat any GPL/AGPL/SSPL dep as a stop-the-line decision. Either swap it for a permissive equivalent or accept the copyleft.

## 4. Patent

### 4.1 Patentability assessment for GroceryApp
- **Barcode scanning** — well-known prior art since 1970s (UPC) and smartphone camera barcode scanning since iOS 11 native API (2017). Not patentable.
- **Expiry-date tracking** — calendar arithmetic. Abstract idea / non-patentable subject matter under both EPO and post-Alice US doctrine.
- **Partial-action quantity splits** — UI/UX flow on top of basic CRUD. Non-patentable on obviousness grounds.
- **Waste analytics** — business-method aggregation. Bjarke v. Alice doctrine in US makes pure software-business-methods extremely hard to patent.
- **Possible exception:** novel ML pipeline for fresh-produce-OCR that doesn't exist in published prior art. Even then, MY/SG/EU have stricter "technical character" requirements than the US.

**Recommendation: do not pursue patents.** Cost (RM 8,000–25,000+ per jurisdiction in fees + agent fees) is wildly disproportionate to enforceability for an indie app.

### 4.2 Defensive disclosure strategy (recommended; free)
- Publish technical blog posts describing novel methods. Date-stamp them via a public timestamping service (e.g. OpenTimestamps via Bitcoin blockchain, free) or simply via a well-archived blog with `web.archive.org` snapshots.
- This establishes **prior art** that prevents others from patenting the same method later. Costs nothing.
- Examples worth disclosing: any novel computer-vision pipeline, any distinctive UX flow that differs from FoodKeeper / Pantry / similar apps.

### 4.3 Cost reference if pursuing MY patent (sanity check, not recommended)
- **MyIPO patent fees** ([MyIPO Patent Forms and Fees](https://www.myipo.gov.my/patent-forms-and-fees/)):
  - Filing (Form 1): RM 290 (with full specification).
  - Substantive examination request: RM 1,100.
  - Grant: RM 660.
  - Annual renewal: RM 270 (Year 2) escalating to RM 1,820 (Year 20).
- **Patent agent fees:** RM 5,000–15,000 per application drafted.
- **Total realistic cost for one MY patent over 20 years:** ~RM 25,000+ before attempting any cross-border PCT extension.

## 5. Trade secret protection

### 5.1 Malaysia — common law, no statute
- Protected under the **common-law tort of breach of confidence** ([Lexology trade secrets in SEA](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=a8e43197-3ae9-493f-aa1b-1c254d4df950); [Azmi & Associates law of confidence](https://www.azmilaw.com/insights/the-law-of-confidence-in-malaysia/)). Three-element test:
  1. Information is confidential in nature.
  2. Communicated in circumstances importing an obligation of confidence.
  3. Used in unauthorised way to detriment of plaintiff.
- **No standalone Trade Secrets Act in Malaysia as of May 2026** ([Rouse SE Asia trade secrets guide 2025](https://rouse.com/insights/news/2025/trade-secrets-protection-in-southeast-asia-a-comprehensive-guide)). The Cyber Security Act 2024 (in force 26 Aug 2024) adds penalties for unauthorised access but does not create a standalone trade-secret right.
- **Statutory edges:** Companies Act 2016 imposes director/employee duties not to misuse company information; Capital Markets and Services Act 2007 covers insider information.

### 5.2 NDA enforceability — MY contractor checklist
- **Mutual NDA** signed before any technical disclosure, with:
  - Defined "Confidential Information" scope (source code, designs, customer lists, business plans).
  - Permitted use limited to the engagement purpose.
  - Term: 2–5 years post-termination is standard and enforceable in MY courts.
  - Return-or-destroy clause on termination.
  - Governing law: Malaysia; jurisdiction: courts of West Malaysia.
- **Non-compete enforceability** — MY courts enforce reasonable post-employment non-competes only narrowly. The Industrial Court is hostile to non-competes that prevent employees from working in their trade for >12 months or beyond a defined geography. **Don't rely on non-competes for MY contractors.** Rely on NDAs + IP assignment.

### 5.3 Practical implementation
- **Source-control hygiene:** private repo (GitHub Pro / GitLab); branch protection on `main`; least-privilege access for contractors (per-repo invites, not org-wide).
- **Separation of secrets:** never commit Firebase service-account keys, API tokens, or secrets to git. Use `.env` + a secret manager (Google Secret Manager / 1Password Secrets Automation).
- **Clean-room procedure** for any logic the founder considers strategically valuable: contractor signs NDA + IP assignment, works in isolated branch, code review on every PR, founder retains final commit rights.
- **Documentation discipline:** mark documents "Confidential" in headers — not legally magical, but supports the "imported obligation of confidence" element of breach-of-confidence claims.

## 6. Domain name defensive strategy

### 6.1 Recommended primary registrations
For brand `<X>` (final brand TBD — see §9):

| Domain | Registrar / TLD | Eligibility | Annual fee (2026, USD/MYR) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `<X>.com` | Any ICANN registrar (Cloudflare, Namecheap, Porkbun) | Open | ~US$10–15 ([ICANN registrar pricing data](https://www.icann.org/en/registrars/find)) |
| `<X>.app` | Any (Google operator) | Open; HTTPS-required (HSTS preload) | ~US$15 |
| `<X>.com.my` | MYNIC accredited reseller (BigDomain, WebServer, etc.) | **Restricted to SSM-registered businesses only** ([MYNIC eligibility for .com.my](https://faq.webnic.cc/kb/my/)) | RM 60 (with SSM)–RM 80 |
| `<X>.my` | MYNIC | Open to MY individuals (NRIC) and entities; foreigners with proof of residence | ~RM 80 |
| `<X>.co` | Any ICANN registrar | Open | ~US$25 |

**Recommendation for indie launch:**
- **Buy `<X>.com` and `<X>.app` immediately** on settling the brand — these are the App Store / Play Store / marketing canonical domains.
- **Buy `<X>.com.my`** because the founder is SSM-registered. RM 60/yr is trivial defensive insurance and signals MY brand provenance to local customers.
- **Skip `<X>.my`, `<X>.co`** at launch unless an obvious typosquat threat exists.

### 6.2 Defensive variants (cost vs benefit)
Common typosquats and homograph variants are tempting but expensive at scale. **Indie rule:** buy at most 2–3 variants of the most likely typo. E.g. if brand is "Pantra" — buy `pantra.com`, `pantra.app`, `pantraapp.com`. Skip plurals, hyphens, regional ccTLDs unless launching there.

### 6.3 UDRP / dispute resolution
If someone squats `<your-brand>.com` after you have a registered trademark, you can recover via WIPO UDRP arbitration:
- **Filing fee:** US$1,500 for 1–5 domains, single panelist; US$2,000 for 6–10 domains ([WIPO UDRP Schedule of Fees](https://www.wipo.int/amc/en/domains/fees/); [WIPO UDRP fee updates](https://www.wipo.int/amc/en/domains/news/2026/news_0005.html)).
- WIPO offers an expedited service at US$4,000 for 1–5 domains.
- Cheaper than litigation, decided in ~60 days.
- **Prerequisite for winning:** registered trademark + bad-faith squat. This is a key reason §1's trademark filing comes before domain disputes.

### 6.4 Sunrise periods
For new gTLDs (e.g. if `.grocery` ever launched), trademark holders get priority registration during a "sunrise" period — but only if registered with the **Trademark Clearinghouse (TMCH)** at ~US$150/yr ([ICANN TMCH](https://newgtlds.icann.org/en/about/trademark-clearinghouse)). Skip TMCH for indie launch; revisit if a brand-relevant new TLD enters sunrise.

## 7. IP in vendor + freelancer contracts

### 7.1 Work-for-hire default — **the Malaysia trap**
- In US copyright, an employee's work made within scope is auto-assigned to the employer (work-for-hire doctrine). **MY does not have an automatic work-for-hire rule for independent contractors.** The Copyright Act 1987 vests copyright in the *author* unless there's a written assignment.
- **Consequence:** if you hire a designer to make the logo and don't get a written assignment, the designer owns the logo. They can re-license it. They can demand payment to release it.
- **Fix:** every contractor agreement (designer, developer, copywriter, video editor) must include an **explicit copyright assignment clause**. Standard language:

  > Contractor hereby irrevocably assigns to Client all worldwide right, title, and interest, including all copyright, in and to the Deliverables, effective on creation. Contractor agrees to execute any further instruments reasonably required to perfect such assignment.

- For employees (when hiring): include the same clause in the employment contract plus a moral-rights waiver to the extent permitted by MY law.

### 7.2 Logo / brand assets commissioned from designers
- Always get a signed **assignment of all IP** before paying the final invoice. Many freelancer platforms (99designs, Fiverr Pro) include this in their default terms — read carefully; some only license, not assign.
- Separately, get the **source files** (Figma, .ai, .psd) — not just exported PNGs.

### 7.3 IP-assignment for any future employees
- Standard MY employment contract should include:
  - Work product assignment clause (prospective).
  - Confidentiality / NDA.
  - Moral-rights waiver.
  - Carve-out for pre-existing IP (employees list any code/projects they exclude).
  - Open-source contribution policy: contributions to OSS during work hours need approval.

### 7.4 Apple / Google trademark usage rules
- **Apple Marketing Resources** ([Apple's Identity Guidelines for Channel Affiliates and Apple-Certified Individuals](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/marketing/guidelines/)): use "Available on the App Store" badge from official assets only, do not modify; never use Apple logo in your own brand; capitalisation rules for "iPhone", "iOS", etc.
- **Google Play Brand Guidelines** ([Google Play developer brand](https://play.google.com/intl/en_us/badges/)): use the "Get it on Google Play" badge only from official assets; never alter colours, proportions; do not use the Android robot logo without permission.
- **Marketing screenshots** must show the actual app, not mock-ups misrepresenting the product.

### 7.5 Stock photos / imagery
- **Pexels, Unsplash, Pixabay** — royalty-free; attribution appreciated but not required under their licences. **Caveat:** people-recognisable in photos may have separate model-release requirements; identifiable buildings/branded products may have separate trademark issues.
- **Shutterstock, Getty, Adobe Stock** — paid licences; track licence IDs per asset; commercial use requires standard or extended licence per their terms.
- **Generative AI images** — copyright status uncertain in many jurisdictions; US Copyright Office has refused registration for purely AI-generated works without human authorship. Treat AI-generated marketing imagery as low-risk for use, but understand you cannot register copyright in it in the US.

## 9. Brand strategy callouts

### 9.1 Pre-clearance trademark search — **before settling on the brand**
Before filing, before printing marketing assets, before submitting to the App Store, search:
- **TMview** ([euipo.europa.eu/eSearch/](https://www.tmdn.org/tmview/)) — free, covers EU + 70+ national offices including MY, SG, ID, TH ([EUIPO TMview AI image search](https://www.euipo.europa.eu/en/news/ai-at-euipo-in-house-image-search-in-tmview-extended-to-all-tm5-offices)).
- **USPTO TM Search** ([tmsearch.uspto.gov](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/)) — replaces the deprecated TESS system in 2026 ([Signa "TESS Is Gone" 2026 update](https://signa.so/blog/tess-is-gone-how-to-search-trademarks)).
- **MyIPO online search** via the IP Online portal.
- **WIPO Global Brand Database** ([branddb.wipo.int](https://branddb.wipo.int/)) — best for international Madrid filings.

**Search iteration:** don't just search exact match. Search homophones, transliterations, and the same logical concept (e.g. for "Pantra" also search "Pantry", "Pantera", "Pantrah"). A clearance search that surfaces nothing in Class 9 + 42 in your target jurisdictions is the green light.

### 9.2 Distinctiveness analysis — **the "GroceryApp" working name is a problem**

The trademark distinctiveness spectrum (USPTO doctrine, mirrored by MyIPO) ([USPTO Strong trademarks](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/strong-trademarks); [USPTO common problems / 2(e) refusal](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/additional-guidance-and-resources/possible-grounds-refusal-mark)):

| Tier | Examples | Registrability for grocery app |
|---|---|---|
| **Generic** | "App for groceries" | **Refused. No registration possible.** |
| **Merely descriptive** | "GroceryApp", "Pantry Tracker" | **Refused on §2(e) / equivalent unless secondary meaning proven** (5+ years exclusive use). |
| **Suggestive** | "FoodKeep", "ShelfLife" | Inherently distinctive. Registrable. |
| **Arbitrary** | "Apple" for computers, "Camel" for cigarettes | Strong. |
| **Fanciful / coined** | "Spotify", "Kodak", "Pantra" | Strongest. Registrable everywhere. |

**"GroceryApp" is at the descriptive tier.** Real-world risk: MyIPO, IPOS, EUIPO, USPTO will all likely object. You'd waste filing fees, delay launch by 6–12 months on an office action, and end up with a mark that — even if it scrapes through — is functionally unenforceable because every competitor can describe their app as a "grocery app" without infringing.

**Strong recommendation: rebrand before filing.** Pick a coined or arbitrary mark. Examples in the same conceptual space (illustrative only — clear via TMview before adopting):
- "Pantra" — coined, suggestive of "pantry"
- "Spiska" — coined, evocative
- "Kibble" / "Larder" — arbitrary but check Class 9/42 conflicts
- "Freshly" — taken in many classes; check
- "Stockd" — coined contraction

This is **the single highest-leverage decision** before launch. Picking a strong mark costs nothing; rebranding after launch costs marketing equity, App Store SKU resubmissions, domain re-purchases, and trademark-refiling fees.

### 9.3 Halal certification (JAKIM) — only for MY food/beverage positioning
- JAKIM Halal certification applies to food, beverages, pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, logistics, and Islamic finance. **It does not apply to non-food digital products** by default ([JAKIM/MYeHALAL system overview](https://www.halaltimes.com/malaysias-halal-certification-goes-fully-digital/)).
- **Relevant only if** the GroceryApp later adds a halal-certified-product filter or a halal verification feature that integrates JAKIM data. If so, partnerships with Verify Halal / Serunai Commerce (the existing JAKIM-endorsed app + SaaS provider) are the standard route, not direct JAKIM certification of the app.
- **Skip for MVP launch.**

## 10. Critical-path actions before launch

Ordered by ROI for an indie sole-proprietor budget. Every one of these is gating; each delivers protection that cannot be retrofit cheaply.

1. **[Cost: free | Effort: 4 hours] Pre-clearance trademark search** on the candidate brand name in TMview + USPTO TM Search + MyIPO online + WIPO Global Brand Database. **DO BEFORE settling on the brand.** Cite: §9.1.
2. **[Cost: free | Effort: 1 day] Rebrand if descriptive.** "GroceryApp" should not be the registered brand. Pick a coined / arbitrary mark and re-verify clearance. Cite: §9.2.
3. **[Cost: ~RM 1,900 | Effort: 1 day filing + 12-mo wait] File MY trademark (Class 9 + Class 42)** at MyIPO. Establishes basic mark for downstream Madrid extension and gives platform-recognised standing for §8 takedowns. Cite: §1.4.
4. **[Cost: ~US$15 + RM 60 = ~RM 130 | Effort: 1 hour] Buy `<brand>.com` + `<brand>.com.my` immediately** on settling the brand. Domain squatters watch new TM applications. Cite: §6.1.
5. **[Cost: free | Effort: ~2 hours per contractor] Add IP assignment + NDA clauses to every contractor agreement** (designer who made logo, any developer hire). MY does not have automatic work-for-hire. Cite: §7.1.
6. **[Cost: free | Effort: 4 hours] Run `license-checker --production` on the React Native + FastAPI dependency tree** and audit for any GPL/AGPL/SSPL leaks. Generate `LICENSES.md` for the in-app Acknowledgements screen. Cite: §3.
7. **[Cost: ~€900 = ~RM 4,500 | Effort: 1 day filing + 4-mo wait] File EU trademark (Class 9 + 42)** at EUIPO once the App Store EU rollout is real. Cheapest per-capita protection in scope (27 countries in one filing). Cite: §1.4.
8. **[Cost: free | Effort: 1 hour] Set up Google Alerts** on brand name + brand-name+app + brand-name+grocery. Cheapest brand-monitoring stack. Cite: §8.4.
9. **[Cost: free | Effort: 1 day] Implement CycloneDX SBOM generation in CI** and a `security@` email + security.txt. Positions for EU CRA reporting (Sep 2026) and full obligations (Dec 2027) without rework. Cite: §3.3.
10. **[Cost: free | Effort: ongoing] Defensive disclosure** — publish technical blog posts on any genuinely novel methods to establish prior art and prevent third-party patenting. Cite: §4.2.

**Skip until traction justifies it:** US trademark (~RM 7,000), Singapore trademark (~RM 1,950), Indonesia/Thailand TM, Madrid Protocol, US Copyright registration, JAKIM halal, brand monitoring SaaS, patents.
